DESIGN HISTORY / 2026-09-19

What changed.
What we cannot yet claim.

A record that keeps three things distinct: visual selection, numerical checks of native CAD, and physical evaluation.

Starting point: assemble it yourself, approximately 180 mm tall.

The original goal was to assemble Mona, Copilot, and Ducky one brick at a time from separate 3D-printed parts. The assumptions were Bambu Lab P1S and PLA, with both 0.2 mm and 0.4 mm nozzles owned. Compatibility with commercial LEGO parts was not a requirement.

The original work comes from a pinned revision of martinwoodward/octoprints. Modifications include uniform scaling, grid discretization, color discretization, splitting and grouping individual parts, custom joints, and visualization and trial data. Attribution and modification details must be retained.

Phase1: three characters × three detail levels

Chunky uses an 8 mm pitch and 4.8 mm brick layers; Balanced uses 6 mm and 3.6 mm; Fine uses 4 mm and 2.4 mm. The following are the individual part counts from that stage.

Visual selection: Mona C / Copilot A / Ducky C

Mona and Ducky were selected in Fine 4 mm, and Copilot in Chunky 8 mm. Character, color, roughly 180 mm scale, and grid-based appearance were selected as visual baselines only. Joint and manufacturing approval remain separate.

Total 26,766 individual instances with unique IDs, sharing 236 native geometry types. This does not mean 26,766 separate STL files were created. The grid envelope and color scheme were retained, but regrouping moved the seams; pixel-for-pixel image identity is not claimed.

The minimum wall at the 4 mm hole entrance increased from the old 0.877 mm to 1.21 mm. The male stud still has a straight stem diameter of 1.10 mm, tip diameter of 0.98 mm, and height of 0.60 mm. Wall thickness or a 0.2 mm nozzle alone cannot establish that the thin PLA stem resists breakage or provides retention.

Preserved files include two shared libraries, three assembly CAD documents with relative links, STEP / STL by type, separate 4 mm and 8 mm trials of 11 parts each (four receivers, four male keys, and three offset-joint parts), and left/right whisker cradles for Mona. The cradles are unnecessary for the initial joint-only trial, but must be placed first in Mona's recorded proposed assembly sequence. Removing them before retention is verified is not approved.

Numerical PASS ≠ physical approval. The archived results for CAD reopening, BRep and meshes, contact graphs, and nominal vertical insertion paths are limited records for their respective revisions. They do not cover real hand or tool access, friction, loads, infill, or insertion/removal durability. The viewer's sequence is not an approved assembly instruction.

First physical trial: small parts and blocked holes

For the 4 mm trial parts, the contributor reported that they were “small and difficult to make” and that “holes were filled with plastic.” The blockage was present before ultrasonic cleaning. Cleaning is not treated as either the cause or a demonstrated solution. See 10 photos, silent videos, and unconfirmed conditions.

Next discussion: common blocks and open underbodies

Following requests to “use real LEGO as a reference” and for “LEGO-like blocks,” discussion is focused on common, easier-to-handle blocks, such as 2×2, 2×4, and 1×2 forms on an approximately 8 mm pitch. The reference principle uses open underbodies, with walls or tubes retaining the studs. Plates or similar parts would add detail only to faces and outlines, separating visual detail from the dimensions of a part that is comfortable to hold. This approximate pitch is not an official LEGO manufacturing tolerance.

No new design has been adopted yet. This publication work has not introduced a new mechanical design or regenerated geometry. It does not guarantee exact preservation of the approximately 180 mm appearance, commercial LEGO compatibility, FDM print suitability, safety, or retention.

  • LEGO: the stud-and-tube principle — The history of the design principle.
  • LDraw dimension specification — Conversion to physical dimensions is approximate, not a manufacturing tolerance.
  • Prusa: FFF printing of LEGO-like parts — Accuracy, first-layer, and fit challenges. Compensation values for other printers are not treated as validated P1S settings.

Third-party images, videos, and models are not republished; only reference links are provided.

Additional wishes for printing options

On 2026-09-20, two printing options were requested: printing parts separately and assembling them afterward, and “printing everything at once.” The second option is awaiting clarification: it could mean a one-piece print requiring no assembly, or batch-printing separate parts on the same plate. One-piece printing has not been confirmed.

The current downloads contain prototype materials for the separate-parts approach only. No one-piece or new batch-printing data has been created or approved. The complete model in the assembly viewer is not treated as directly printable in one piece. This Japanese/English localization work does not regenerate geometry, slice, or print; full-kit printing remains on hold.

Scope of public-copy cleanup

Only the enumerated deliverables were copied. The development repository's history, runtime environment, conversations, internal logs, and caches are not included. The viewer was ported from known committed source, using public relative URLs and a fixed status record. No analytics reporting, advertising, external fonts, or CDNs are used.

Ancillary JSON metadata, such as execution commands and local paths, was removed. Blender's native API was used to make stored file-browser and render-output paths relative; matching geometry/placement fingerprints and reopening were checked. For FreeCAD, only XML document properties inside the file container were cleaned up, preserving BRep and other geometry streams and relative library references. Autosave backups are not included.

Thumbnails are reduced versions of actual generated images, not invented promotional images. Photos and physical-trial videos use only previously published files with personal metadata removed; original attachments, audio, and sensor tracks are not published.

The pre-copy inventory of authorized files and the public-file hash inventory distinguish original hashes from public-derivative hashes. Historical verification files record hashes of the inputs checked at that time. Use the public inventory to verify current downloads. The native-file cleanup and geometry-preservation record is also included.
